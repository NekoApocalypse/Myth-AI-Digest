## The Headline

**Cognition ships SWE-2, their first multi-trillion-parameter RL coding model.** Post-trained from Kimi K3 (2.8T MoE), it hits 50.0% on FrontierCode 1.1 — within 1 point of Fable 5.1 at 64% lower cost, and beats it on Terminal-Bench 2.1 (92.8 vs 91.4). The real story is the training method: a single-run RL objective with cost-penalized rewards that pushes the entire Pareto frontier at once. SWE-2 medium out-scores SWE-1.7 while taking 58% fewer turns. The catch: Terminal-Bench 4 (long-horizon) sits at 27.3 — still miles behind GPT-6 Astra's 57.9. The gap between "do this task" and "run this project" is where the frontier actually lives. <https://cognition.com/blog/swe-2>

## The Stack

[Agents] **OpenAI Agents API goes live** — OpenAI's managed Codex harness, now a first-class API. Sessions, sandboxes, MCP server connections, subagent delegation, context compaction, and recovery — all handled by OpenAI. Your app provides tools and picks the execution environment (OpenAI-hosted or self-hosted sandbox). This is the production-grade agent runtime they've been building toward. If you were rolling your own agent harness, you now have a decision to make. <https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/agents-api/overview>

[Architecture] **AI-generated Lean 4 formal proofs drop verification cost by ~10,000x** — OpenAI's Navier-Stokes proof shipped with a machine-verifiable Lean 4 formalization, verified in 17 hours. The old rule of thumb was 40 person-hours per page of undergrad math. A 166-page research paper at 20x density = ~133K hours. This is four orders of magnitude cheaper, and it's not just math — formally verifying security policies, smart contracts, and mission-critical algorithms suddenly has a plausible ROI. <https://www.johndcook.com/blog/2026/09/09/formal-method-revolution/>

[Figures] **Anthropic's Sept 2026 threat intel report drops** — Covers Dec 2025–Aug 2026 across cyber ops, surveillance, influence ops, bio misuse, and illicit distillation. Key takeaway: sophisticated attacks no longer require sophisticated attackers. AI-driven autonomous cyber kill chains have proliferated from state actors to lone individuals using open-source frameworks like PentAGI. Notable: Claude Fable/Mythos models were used in zero malicious cases (except one distillation incident), suggesting model-level safeguards are actually holding. <https://www.anthropic.com/threat-intelligence-report-september-2026>

[Frontier Models] **OpenAI's Navier-Stokes victory lap hits a trust landmine** — Mathematicians are publicly asking whether OpenAI's proof used unpublished researcher data from chat sessions. OpenAI says no specific user data was accessed for the proof but "cannot rule out that de-identified data derived from their usage helped improve models." Combined with allegations that OpenAI pressured a researcher to drop an Anthropic-affiliated coauthor from a paper, the trust damage is real: HN comments are full of companies saying they're revisiting whether to give closed-model labs *any* data at all. If this accelerates enterprise self-hosting of open models, it's a self-inflicted wound with long-term structural consequences. <https://mathstodon.xyz/@andreasthom/117240535270608201>
