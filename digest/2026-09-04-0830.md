**[Frontier Models] GPT-6 Astra on ARC-AGI-3** — ARC Prize's independent analysis: 62.7% with standard harness ($26K), 99.9% with provider adapter ($19K). Also built per-game toolchains (parsers, planners, search) in a code sandbox. The action-efficiency parity with humans is the real story here, not the score. <https://arcprize.org/blog/astra>

**[Hardware] Qwen 3.8 27B on Cerebras at 1500 tok/s** — Wafer-scale inference serving this at genuinely interactive speeds. **[Agents] Which tools do coding agents actually pick?** — Armature ran 16,893 sessions across Claude Code, Codex, and Cursor. Result: agents disagree on tool selection 58% of the time. Getting cited ≠ getting installed. <https://armature.tech/blog/which-tools-coding-agents-install>

## The Interesting Bits

**[Agents] Zed: Xanadu Was Waiting for Agents** — Zed's blog connects Ted Nelson's 1965 Xanadu vision (permanent versioning, transclusion by reference, never-delete) to DeltaDB's CRDT + Merkle tree implementation. The thesis: agents are the first users who can actually *use* a fully lossless, fragment-addressable code history. They can follow every link, every time. Humans couldn't; agents can. "Sixty years is a long time for an idea to wait for its users. They're here now." <https://zed.dev/blog/agentic-xanadu>

**[Breakout] Porting a 1993 Amiga game with an LLM reading 68000 assembly** — Dev handed Claude Fable 5 72,758 lines of uncommented 68000 assembly from his 1993 game. It reproduced byte-identical binaries, reverse-engineered undocumented map formats, and discovered that doors weren't tile data — they were runtime-drawn objects the author had misremembered for 33 years. The model built its own testing harness with `--drive`, `--probe`, and `--screenshot` flags. This is the kind of thing that makes you stop and stare. <https://babyloniantwins.com/blog/porting-a-1993-amiga-game-to-godot/>

**[Breakout] Go grandmaster Shin beats KataGo with 2-stone handicap** — Shin Jin-seo won 2-1 against KataGo (4x RTX 3090, 20s/move = ~100K playouts). Strategy: exploit KataGo's training limitation — it optimizes for even games and refuses sub-0.0005-point moves even when they'd bait a weaker opponent. Shin played ultra-solid, forced long joseki sequences, and never burned through his full handicap buffer. KataGo routinely beats pros with 3-4 stones, so this is a genuine tactical insight, not just a human bounce. <https://www.kedglobal.com/artificial-intelligence/newsView/ked202607210007>
