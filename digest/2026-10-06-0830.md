Competitive with GLM 5.2 and Qwen 3.8-Max on coding/agentic tasks while burning 3–4× less inference compute. ## The Stack

**[Architecture] Dust: Pretraining Transformers Without Backpropagation** — Q Labs (backed by Jeff Dean) publishes the first zeroth-order method competitive with backprop for LM pretraining. Instead of weight-space evolution, it perturbs activations per-token, treating each token as a virtual population member — 10³–10⁴× more efficient than weight-space ES. Larger models are *more* population-efficient. At scale it exceeds backprop. No backward pass required. Code: <https://github.com/qlabs-eng/dust> | Paper: <https://qlabs.sh/research/dust>

**[Breakout] Claude Opus 5.5 agents discover two room-temp magnetic semiconductors** — Vals AI ran Opus 5.5 agents through quantum simulations and found two Luttinger-compensated antiferromagnets suitable for next-gen spintronics memory. One is novel (YBaMnFeO₅), the other (KV[Cr(CN)₆]) was synthesized in 1999 but nobody noticed its spin-sorting properties until an AI agent pointed it out. All computations, raw outputs, and a one-command checker are open on GitHub. <https://www.vals.ai/blogs/room-temperature-magnetic-semiconductors>

**[Hardware] Qualcomm licenses Huawei's LogicFolding chip patents** — Multi-year cross-license deal covering 5G, compute, AI, and networking patents. Qualcomm also purchased certain Huawei U.S. patents in compute and AI. The LogicFolding tech is a novel chip architecture from Huawei — despite sanctions, their IP portfolio is now generating revenue from Qualcomm. The agreement needs regulatory approval to close. <https://www.huawei.com/en/news/2026/10/qualcomm-broad-patent-agreement>

**[Agents] Apple and a Hacker's Future** — Ben Thompson's headless Mac Mini agent host got pwned via CVE-2026-65400 (macOS screen sharing vuln). His Claude agent detected the intrusion, halted all commands unilaterally, and helped root out the malware. Meanwhile Apple announced tighter Full Disk Access restrictions explicitly targeting AI agents. The irony: TCC permission prompts (GUI-only, invisible to software) forced him to keep screen sharing open, which created the attack surface. Long read but the core tension is real — macOS was the perfect agent host, and Apple is systematically making it worse for exactly that use case. <https://stratechery.com/2026/apple-and-a-hackers-future/>
