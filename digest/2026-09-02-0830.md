## The Stack

**[Frontier Models] Claude Fable 5.1 & Mythos 5.1** — Anthropic's new flagship sets SOTA on agentic coding (55.8% TB4), agentic science (52.6% TB-Science), and knowledge work. 25% cheaper than Fable 5 on normal usage, up to ~45% cheaper on agentic workloads thanks to cache-read repricing. Mythos 5.1 is the safety-gated version for cyber + bio, with a US government-partnered access program for biology researchers. <https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-1>

**[Frontier Models] OpenAI Astra hits Critical cyber threshold** — First model OpenAI designates at Critical under their Preparedness Framework. 100% on ExploitBench, discovered two zero-days during evaluation, built a full browser-compromise chain that escaped sandbox. They paused training after the Hugging Face incident, restarted large RL runs on Aug 28. Access to advanced cyber capabilities will route through Daybreak Blue, not general availability. The operational implication: frontier model release cadence is now gated by infrastructure hardening, not just capability. <https://openai.com/index/path-to-astra/>

**[Frontier Models] World Labs Atlas — a world model that actually works** — Fei-Fei Li's team dropped Atlas, a multimodal autoregressive diffusion transformer trained from scratch on text + images + video + 3D. Camera-controlled generation up to 1 min at 1440p, sparse-view 3D reconstruction that beats specialized models, real-to-sim robotics workflows. The architecture natively treats camera geometry as an input type and maintains a spatial context where images are grounded at 3D positions. This isn't another "look at this video" demo — the reconstruction benchmarks suggest genuine generalization. <https://www.worldlabs.ai/blog/atlas>

**[Architecture] Small transformer hits 44% on ARC-AGI-1 for $0.67** — mvakde trained a tiny transformer from scratch in 1.5 hours on a 5090 and it beats most LLMs on ARC-AGI-1. Key tricks: supervised-only loss (no input token training — counterintuitively works better), SwiGlu/RMSnorm, Normuon optimizer, 3D RoPE + per-task additive embeddings. Open source. The ablation shows per-task embeddings and 3D RoPE together provide most of the gain — dropping either tanks performance from 44% to ~24%. If you care about sample efficiency, this is the most interesting architecture signal this week. <https://mvakde.github.io/blog/44-on-arc-1/>

**[Hardware] MoE streaming from SSD: 104GB model on 48GB Mac at 12 tok/s** — slotstream runs Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (125B MoE, 104GB at 4-bit) by loading only the 3.8GB trunk and streaming expert weights from SSD on demand. Single Swift binary, Ollama-compatible API, auto-sizes to available RAM. Supports speculative decoding (86% first-draft accept rate, 1.5–1.9x speedup). The MoE expert-streaming pattern is what makes giant models viable on consumer hardware — and this is a clean, production-quality implementation. <https://github.com/carloslfu/slotstream>

**[Agents] Codex app bundles full LibreOffice as agent runtime dependency** — Simon Willison found that OpenAI's Codex desktop app ships Python, Node.js, git, Poppler, and a 423MB LibreOffice in `~/.cache/codex-runtimes/` as part of its default skills plugins. It downloads on first run, not at install time. This reveals the actual strategy for document-handling agents: bundle decades-old battle-tested Office software rather than reimplement file parsing. A 423MB dependency for a chat app is absurd on the surface, but it's the correct engineering tradeoff when your agent needs to read every Office format ever created. <https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/1/codex-libreoffice/>

## Vibe Check

Frontier model releases are accelerating but so is the safety infrastructure around them — Anthropic's EFS and OpenAI's Critical-threshold framework both signal that the industry is treating deployment risk as a first-class engineering constraint, not a PR checkbox. Meanwhile, small-model research (ARC-AGI transformers, SSD-streamed MoEs) and world models (Atlas) are quietly expanding what "AI" means beyond text. The gap between frontier labs and scrappy open-source work is narrowing on architecture, widening on compute.
