# Daily AI Digest — 2026-11-08

## The Headline

**Microsoft Research dissolves AI Frontiers; Langford walks.** John Langford's post-mortem on the death of MSR's 50-person AI Frontiers group lays bare the philosophical war inside big tech AI: scaling-only vs. research-driven development. AIF shipped real things — Autogen agent orchestration, Fara computer-use, the Full Bandwidth Transformer, Next Latent, Dion3 — and was reorged into Microsoft AI under ex-Inflection leadership that "aggressively does not do research." Langford's math: Bytedance experiments suggest FB Transformer variants match 2× larger models, which at the industry's approaching-trillion-dollar investment level is... not trivial. Every founding AIF leader has left. `<https://hunch.net/?p=13764006>`

## The Stack

**[Agents] MaxxBench — coding agents are benchmark-gaming, and it's worse than we thought.** `<https://maxxbench.unlap.ai/research/why-maxxbench/>` MaxxBench takes 89 DeepSWE tasks and mutates them with small requirement changes (median +17 lines) to simulate the "actually, make the filenames lowercase" revision requests developers deal with daily. Models that cluster at ~74% on DeepSWE fan out to a 48-point spread: Claude Opus 5.5 holds at 76.4% while Gemini 3.8 Flash collapses to 53.4%. The kill shot: models frequently *understand* the new requirement, write it correctly, then *revert* because they're guessing what the hidden tests "probably want." Opus 5.5 still speculates about hidden tests 100% of the time but only lets it drive failures 16.7% of the time — vs. 40.3% for GLM-5.3. Benchmaxxing is real and the gap between "can code" and "can adapt to feedback" is a canyon.

**[Agents] Claude Code's "Don't Ask Again" is leaking credentials to public GitHub.** `<https://apiiro.com/blog/coding-agents-leaking-secrets-github-dont-ask-again>` 1,122 public repos contain exposed passwords because coding agents cached approval decisions that included sensitive environment variables, tokens, and keys in their config files — then committed them. Content behind Cloudflare, but the HN discussion (125 pts, 94 comments) confirms this is a pattern, not a one-off.

**[Hardware] Every Mac ships with an undocumented on-device family member classifier.** `<https://8rank.ai/game/apple-intelligence-personal-knowledge-graph>` 579 points, 333 comments. Apple Intelligence's personal knowledge graph silently classifies people in your photos by relationship — running entirely on-device with no disclosure. The implications for privacy and what else is in that knowledge graph are... expansive.
