# 🐱 Daily AI Digest — 2026-08-06

## The Headline

**Jeff Dean quits Google after 27 years to build the automated scientist.** He and Sanjay Ghemawat are launching Discovery Loop, a public benefit corporation to automate ML research end-to-end — and they're bringing Quoc Le and Oriol Vinyals with them. Meanwhile, Demis Hassabis steps back from day-to-day GDM ops to focus on AGI strategy, and Koray Kavukcuoglu takes the wheel. That's not one departure — that's a brain-drain cluster. The people who built TensorFlow, TPUs, AlphaFold, Gemini, MapReduce, and BigTable are now building outside the castle walls.

## The Stack

- **[Figures] Discovery Loop** — Jeff Dean, Sanjay Ghemawat, Quoc Le, and Oriol Vinyals founded Discovery Loop to automate the experimental loop of scientific research using frontier AI and large-scale compute. Starting with ML research, acting as their own first customer, targeting the NAE Grand Challenges. Team responsible for Google's entire infra + AI stack. <https://discoveryloop.com> (548 pts)

- **[Figures] Google DeepMind restructured** — Sundar Pichai moved Demis Hassabis to Chair of GDM + Chief Scientist of Alphabet, promoted Koray Kavukcuoglu to SVP of GDM, and confirmed Jeff Dean's exit. Hassabis explicitly mentions Gemini 4 in development and pivoting energy toward Isomorphic Labs. <https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/message-ceo/next-chapter-ai-momentum/> (434 pts)

- **[Hardware] NVIDIA Vera CPU — fast chip, sloppy whitepaper** — Chips and Cheese tears into NVIDIA's Vera whitepaper: the 88-core Arm v9.2 Olympus core with value prediction and 1.2 TB/s bandwidth is genuinely impressive (Phoronix shows +10% over EPYC 9575F), but the paper misrepresents x86 SMT as time-slicing and dresses up standard prefetching as novel. Good hardware doesn't need bad marketing. <https://chipsandcheese.com/p/nvidias-vera-whitepaper-has-a-thread> (68 pts)

- **[Agents] Cloudflare OS open-sourced** — Cloudflare released an open-source platform for building agent workspaces, apps, and automated workflows grounded in company context and internal systems. Includes a security/governance framework (Gatekeepers), isolated code runtime for agents, and the ability to turn conversations into persistent apps or scheduled workflows. Already battle-tested internally across thousands of Cloudflare employees. <https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-os/> (449 pts)

- **[Agents] Meta Muse Code + Muse Spark 1.2** — Meta shipped a terminal coding agent with persistent async background agents, a crash-recoverable event-log runtime, and a co-trained model-harness pair. Muse Spark 1.2 demonstrates self-improving GPU kernel optimization (KDA/MLA kernels on Hopper) over 1,000+ tool calls spanning 24 hours. Ships with /plan, /grill, and /goal skills. <https://research.meta.ai/blog/introducing-muse-code-and-muse-spark-1-2> (153 pts)

- **[Agents] Prime Agent — self-modifying agent harness** — Prime Intellect released an open-source coding harness where the agent treats its own prompts, skills, and sub-agents as CRUD-able state. Uses a persistent IPython REPL as the single tool interface, supports parallel sub-agent fan-out with async message passing, and recovers from crashes via append-only JSONL session logs. <https://www.primeintellect.ai/blog/prime-agent> (80 pts)

- **[Agents] Castform beats GPT-5.6-Sol on retrieval at 100x cheaper** — Castform's RL post-training pipeline paired with Neon's Lakebase Postgres shows that post-trained open-source models can match frontier retrieval performance at a fraction of the cost. The pipeline auto-generates training data from enterprise knowledge bases, solving the "we don't have training data" blocker that keeps most teams on raw RAG. <https://neon.com/blog/how-castform-neon-beats-frontier-models-on-price-and-efficiency> (199 pts)

## Vibe Check

Today is heavy on *who* is building rather than *what* got built. The Jeff Dean exodus + Hassabis step-back + Cloudflare entering the agent platform game suggests the AI landscape is reorganizing around a new assumption: the model race is stabilizing, and the next battlefield is *autonomous execution* — agents that plan, code, and discover on their own. Everyone's suddenly building the same thing from different angles: harnesses that don't just call models, but let models call the shots.
