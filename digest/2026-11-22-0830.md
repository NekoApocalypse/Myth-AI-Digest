## 🎯 The Headline

**Inkling 2 Mini dropped.** Thinking Machines Lab shipped a 26.4B-total / 3.4B-active looped MoE that runs on a MacBook. Not "in theory runs" — their researchers built a playable flight simulator on a flight with it, offline on an M5 Pro. Middle layers loop 3× per token: more reasoning without more CoT tokens. Open weights, NVFP4 native. Leads every open-weights model under 100B on DeepSWE v1.1 (48.2%) and Vals Index v2.2 (46.5%). This is the first looped-transformer release that's both practical and open. (<https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/>)

## 📡 The Stack

**[Hardware] LookAtMyQuant: Inkling 2 Mini compressed to 5.4 GB** — A quantization compiler using trellis codes + Fisher-weighted bit allocation. 12× lower KL than llama.cpp's Q4_K_M at equal size. Compresses Inkling 2 Mini to 5.38 GB at 1.65 bpp and still hits 88% GSM8K. At 12.76 GB it matches the BF16 teacher on every benchmark: zero degradation. MIT-licensed, Apple Silicon MLX runtimes. The gap between "cloud model" and "runs on my laptop" just collapsed. (<https://github.com/ShinMegamiBoson/lookatmyquant>)

**[Architecture] LLMs read and write executables byte by byte from hex dumps** — Sol 6.1 reads ELF64 binaries as raw hex, identifies programs, patches them at the byte level, and writes fully functional executables from scratch. No compiler, no assembler, no disassembler. Found a real bug in FreeBSD's ministat (wrong confidence-interval critical values) from the bytes alone. Correctly identified a malware sample's C2 server from a stripped PE32+. The limiting factor is context — ~2.4 tokens per byte means 67 KB costs ~163K tokens. But the implication is clear: for an LLM, a hex dump is just text. (<https://sgiannopoulos.github.io/blog/machine-code-programming/>)

**[Agents] Pandex — European law API + MCP server** — 2.8M official legal documents across EU + 7 countries, served over REST and MCP for AI agents to ground legal answers with real citations. Semantic cross-language search. Integrates with Claude, Cursor, Copilot. The thin-wrapper signal flashes here (18 points on HN), but the MCP integration is genuine infrastructure — this is the kind of tool that makes agents legally actionable rather than hallucination-generators. (<https://www.pandex.io>)

## 🔇 The Noise

**Pandex** is pulling its weight today mostly by being the only agent-tool item on an otherwise slow day. It's an API over legal documents with an MCP shim. Useful, but it's not novel infrastructure — it's a data product with MCP as a distribution channel. Worth watching if the corpus coverage expands beyond the current 7 member states, but at 18 points the community is correctly calibrating it as "nice, not groundbreaking."
