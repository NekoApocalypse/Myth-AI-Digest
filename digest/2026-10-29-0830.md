# Daily AI Digest — 2026-10-29

**The Headline:** Google dropped **Gemini 4 Carbon** — 15 days after Argon — and it's a real step change: #1 on Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5.0 (59, ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 at 58), built for multi-day agentic engineering runs and the full cyber-defense lifecycle.

**The Stack:**

[Frontier Models] **Gemini 4 Carbon** — Google's next frontier model, tuned hard for sustained agentic coding and cybersecurity. SEC-bench Pro 88.2% (vuln finding), ExploitGym 54.8% (exploitation), CWE-bench 71.0% (patching), 61.2% on FrontierSWE V2. Long-horizon is the story: internal teams report it running for days to root-cause subtle bugs across large codebases. <https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-carbon/>

[Architecture] **Cartesia "From Tokens to Bytes" — H-Nets** — Byte-level hierarchical networks that replace (rather than merely shrink) tokenization, chunking data dynamically inside the model. A matched 1-stage H-Net hits the BPE baseline's loss with 1.28x less compute, widening to ~2x on evals and up to 5.37x on out-of-domain code (Prolog). A 2-stage H-Net beats a matched open model with ~10x less compute *and* data. The first serious architectural challenge to BPE tokenizers I've seen backed by compute-optimal scaling laws. <https://www.cartesia.ai/blog/from-tokens-to-bytes-part-1>

[Frontier Models] **NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe** — Open-weight, promptable ASR in a single 30B checkpoint (4B active, Mamba–Transformer MoE): transcribes 40 languages, translates 31, diarizes 13, plus audio Q&A. #1 open-weight on HF Open ASR (5.33% WER English) and 2.8% AA-WER at ~1/6 the active params of its nearest peer. Ships with Granary v2, an open 463k-hour / 48-language curation pipeline — genuinely reproducible, not just a leaderboard flex. <https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/nemotron-transcribe-granary>

[Agents] **Cursor Agent SDK** — Agents defined as TypeScript, deployed with `agent-sdk deploy`, with evals and event replay out of the box. It's the framework behind their own Bugbot / Security Reviewer / Rollouts. The signal: agents are becoming "code you ship and review," not chat UIs — build-around-your-workflow, not adopt-someone-else's. <https://cursor.com/blog/agent-sdk>

[Agents] **Surge AI DAYJOB: Tax** — A benchmark of 50 real tax assignments (1,789 workspace files) that tests whether agents flag *consequential issues the client never asked about*. Leader: Claude Sonnet 5.5 at a brutal **10.8%** pass rate. This is the honest counterweight to the benchmark bragging above. <https://surgehq.ai/blog/dayjob-tax>

**The Noise:** Carbon is the headline, but you can't actually run it — gated to a curated "Fairwind Program" of cyber defenders with no public API date. Announced ≠ available. And it's a closed model with no technical report or parameter count yet, so treat the security benchmark numbers as marketing until third parties reproduce them.

**Vibe Check:** The frontier is sprinting (two Gemini flagships in 15 days, byte-level architectures fighting the tokenizer), but DAYJOB's 10.8% is the reality check — raw capability is climbing faster than *professional judgment*.
