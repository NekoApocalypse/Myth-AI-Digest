# Daily AI Digest — 2026-10-24

## The Headline

Moonshot AI dropped **Kimi K3.1 Pro** — a 3T-class open-weight model that goes toe-to-toe with Claude Opus 5.5 on agentic coding benchmarks (65.7% vs 66.4% on Terminal-Bench 4.0). Open weights land Nov 4. This is the kind of release that actually moves the needle. 😼

## The Stack

- **[Frontier Models] Kimi K3.1 Pro** — Moonshot's latest, post-trained from K3 with large-scale RL across hundreds of domains. 1M-token context window. Strong across coding, 3D creation, and financial research. The interesting bit: they optimized the entire RL stack so every compute increment delivers predictable returns. Also noteworthy — they explicitly removed cyber-related training data to cap offensive capability at 81% on xdev-bench-hard (down from 90.5%), which is a concrete safety lever worth watching. Open weights by Nov 4. <https://www.kimi.ai/blog/kimi-k3-1-pro>

- **[Frontier Models] Dessa** — Block (Jack Dorsey's company) built a full-duplex speech-to-speech model trained from scratch on nothing but human conversation audio. No text. No pretrained models. Zero. Trained on a single NVIDIA GB200 NVL72 rack, runs locally on a MacBook Pro M4 Max. This is a genuinely different bet — native speech modeling without the text bottleneck, claiming to understand timing, emotion, laughter, and pauses natively. Waitlist only for now, early access for safety researchers. No benchmarks published yet, so grain of salt. <https://block.xyz/dessa>

- **[Architecture] ThinkingCap Gemma-4-26B-A4B** — Bottlecap AI fine-tuned Google's Gemma-4 MoE to use 38.9% fewer thinking tokens on average (up to 69% less on LongBench v2) with only 0.15pp accuracy cost across 12 benchmarks. Looping dropped from 3.6% to 0.8%. Same hardware, same sampling settings, drop-in replacement. The real win here is inference cost savings without touching model architecture — open weights on HuggingFace including GGUF builds. <https://bottlecapai.com/post/thinkingcap-gemma-4-26b/>

## Vibe Check

Open-weight models are closing the gap with proprietary flagships faster than most expected. Kimi K3.1 Pro nearly matches Opus 5.5 on agentic tasks, Block is betting on native speech models skipping text entirely, and inference efficiency keeps improving with techniques like ThinkingCap. The frontier is getting both more capable and more distributed — and the compute-to-capability ratio keeps shifting in interesting ways.
