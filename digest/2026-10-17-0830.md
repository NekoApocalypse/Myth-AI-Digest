## The Headline

**Claude cracked a 25-year-old cryptographic mystery** by discovering that NIST's ECDSA curve seeds weren't cleverly hashed passphrases — they were just `lrand48()` seeded with Unix timestamps from 1997–1998. Turns out the most suspicious-looking crypto standard in history was generated by a bog-standard C library PRNG. Sometimes the boring answer is the right one, and the bounty ($36,864) goes to the Internet Security Research Group. This actually *increases* confidence the curves aren't backdoored.

## The Stack

**[Frontier Models] FLUX 3 Image Klein** — Black Forest Labs dropped a 5.6B open-weights image model that outperforms larger competitors (Qwen-Image 2.1 at 7B, Krea 2 at 12.9B) in text-to-image, editing, and multi-reference generation. Runs on 8GB VRAM GPUs via INT8, ~4s per 1MP image on an RTX 5090. Bounding-box control for placement and editing. Open weights under FLUX Kommunity License. 🐱 <https://bfl.ai/blog/flux-3-image-klein>

**[Hardware] Apple: Muse Spark Open (816B MoE) runs locally across 4 Mac Studios** — Apple ML Research demoed distributed inference of a near-trillion-parameter model over Thunderbolt 5 RDMA using MLX. Pipeline parallelization across 2 machines, tensor parallelization across 4. 36 tok/s decode at NVFP4 precision. M5 Ultra's GPU Neural Accelerator delivers up to 4.6× faster TTFT vs M3 Ultra. Peak power: ~300W per Mac Studio — less than a toaster. <https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/mlx-distributed-inference>

**[Hardware] llama.cpp on a jailbroken PS5** — PS5LM loads GGUF models onto the console's RDNA 2 GPU (11.4 GB usable), serves OpenAI/Anthropic-compatible APIs over the network, and even runs decision models (d1, Clef) via Ollaya API. Qwen 3.5 9B hits 55 tok/s; Qwen 3.8 27B at ~20 tok/s. Completely local, no cloud, no PC required while running. Also supports vision models. Pure "your console, your model server" energy. <https://github.com/cobanov/PS5LM>

**[Agents] StarSkirmish Bench v0.2 — LLMs write StarCraft bots** — Updated benchmark testing flagship models (GPT-6 Astra, Claude Opus 5.5, Gemini 4 Argon) coding Protoss bots in C++ with 1 hour of wall clock time. GPT-6 Astra leads; the gap between models and top human-written bots (Stardust, PurpleWave) remains massive — best model wins only 7% of games against them. The strongest predictor of score: how much the model practiced against strong bots. <https://starskirmish.com/bench/v0-2/>

**[Architecture] Building transformers from associative memory first principles** — Alex Wang derives how linear layers → MLPs → self-attention emerge naturally when you think about memory stores: outer-product accumulation (capacity ~d_k), optimal least-squares recall (2× capacity), and selective nonlinear recall (exponential capacity jump). Interactive post with draggable parameters. Clean pedagogical work — if you've ever wanted the "attention is just associative memory" argument made rigorous. <https://alexwang.ai/posts/associate-memory/>

## The Noise

**Anthropic's quantum circuit design with Claude** (88 pts) — Claude Mythos 5 redesigned 8 quantum circuit primitives, improving on best published designs by up to 12×. Impressive from an "AI autonomously doing research" angle, but the practical impact depends entirely on quantum hardware that's still years away. Cool benchmark, thin on immediate implications. <https://www.anthropic.com/research/designing-efficent-quantum-circuits>

**Agent harness prompt caching** (34 pts) — Neuralwatt measured how agent harnesses (OpenCode, Pi, omp) waste energy by breaking prompt caches: a cache miss ~2x energy, and past 128k context, 3 in 1,000 turs re-read half the prompt from scratch. Every cache break they reproduced was a harness bug, not a model issue. Fixable, but revealing about how sloppy agent infra still is. <https://neuralwatt.com/news/why-harnesses-matter-for-enegy-eficiency>
