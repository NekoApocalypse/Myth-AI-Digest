## The Stack

- **[Agents] Real-SWE: Benchmarking AI models on private, real-world enterprise codebases** (<https://withspecific.com/benchmarks/real-swe>) — The first benchmark using licensed private production codebases (billing, tax logic, multi-service migrations). Claude Code + Fable 5.1 leads at 38.8%, GPT-6 Astra at 33.8%, Gemini 3.8 Flash at 31.2%. Median instruction is 1,742 chars and 11 files edited — substantially harder than FrontierCode or DeepSWE. Agents fail at a ~72% rate even on short rollouts. If you trusted SWE-bench numbers, this will sober you up.

- **[Hardware] Getting 50 GB/s Back from the Apple Neural Engine** (<https://eiln.github.io/posts/ane-dma.html>) — Brutal piece of silicon archaeology. An M3 ANE RTL erratum throttles DRAM weight streaming from 45–60 GB/s down to 17–19 GB/s whenever weight tensor sizes hit multiples of 1 MiB (which they do, often). The author identified the kernel DMA speculative prefetch ring as the culprit and worked around it, taking Llama 3.2 1B from 10.0 → 24.3 tok/s and Qwen3-8B from 1.36 → 2.97 tok/s. Pure black-box reverse-engineering at its finest.

- **[Hardware] Retrospectively Reverse-Engineering Apple's Neural Engine** (<https://eiln.github.io/posts/ane.html>) — Companion piece from the same author. Full architectural map of the M1 ANE: 16 cores × 128 FP16 MAC lanes, Q16.16 fixed-point accumulator saturating at 2¹⁵, pointwise activation fused post-MAC without DRAM round-trip. The real insight is in the dataflow assumptions — the ANE was architected for CNN-era predictable reuse patterns, which is why M5 folded ANE cores into the GPU. A useful postmortem on Apple's NPU bet.

- **[Architecture] A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits (2021)** (<https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html>) — The Anthropic interpretability team's foundational paper resurfaced on HN (78 pts). Classic but still the reference for anyone working on mech interp. Content unavailable at fetch time, but you've read this one.
