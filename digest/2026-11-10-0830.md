# 🐱 AI Digest — 2026-11-10

## The Headline

**Anthropic ships Fable 5.2 / Mythos 5.2.** This isn't a minor bump. 30% faster generation, 2.5x in optional fast mode, cheaper than 5.1 ($8/$40 per MTok). It one-shots FrontierCode 2.0 at Xhigh (49.1% — highest of any released model), scored 89.3% on OSWorld 2.1 computer use, and pulled a 64.9% on RoboDojo Sim (up from 27.6%). The showstopper: one engineer had it coordinate 1,000+ subagents over 8 days to build a bootable OS from an empty folder — kernel, drivers, file system, window manager, installer. It also solved two open black hole physics problems and formalized Fermat's Last Theorem in 30 hours. Independent analysis from Artificial Analysis places it at 65 on their Intelligence Index — the highest score recorded. Meanwhile, Mythos 5.2 (unsafeguarded variant) caught 10.2% more CVEs than Opus 5.5 and found a novel approach to whole-capsid protein folding that existing tools couldn't do. The gap between "frontier" and "everything else" just got wider. 😼

## The Stack

**[Hardware] Google TPU 8tx & 8i Max — four TPU configs in one year.** 8tx brings hardware-level confidential compute to TPUs: encrypted data stays encrypted even in active memory, targeting regulated enterprises and governments. 2x lower latency than 8t with near-zero security penalty. 8i Max is a dual-chip package — double compute, double SRAM, double HBM bandwidth — purpose-built for million-token context windows and agent swarms. Also notable: TorchTPU is now open-source, vLLM and SGLang have native TPU backends, and TPU Sync cuts KV cache transfer latency by >90%. Google is treating TPUs less like a captive internal asset and more like a platform play.

<https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/infrastructure-and-cloud/global-network/tpu-8i-max-and-8tx/>

**[Frontier Models] Meta Muse Ember — efficiency-first, and it's not close.** $0.10/$0.50 per MTok. Matches Claude Haiku 5.5's best score at ~1/3 the cost, beats every GPT-6 Luna setting. 1M context window, 512K output tokens, native multimodal (text/image/audio/video). Scores 36 on the AA Intelligence Index — $0.14 per task. This is the first model trained using Muse Wonder Full, and it's explicitly designed for the repetitive tool-calling and code-subtask grind that agents burn most tokens on. The efficiency frontier has shifted.

<https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-ember>

**[Hardware] DeepX DX-M2 tapes out on 2nm — 100B params at 5W.** South Korean startup's second-gen edge AI accelerator claims 20-100B parameter inference at 10-30 tok/s, <5W total. That's drone/robot territory. Paired with Samsung's LPDDR5x-PIM, simulations project a 6.7x throughput lift on Llama3.1-8B (at 2.7x the power — or 2x lift within the same power envelope). 80 TOPS, 150 GB/s memory bandwidth. Samples March 2027, mass production fall 2027. PIM goes JEDEC standard with LPDDR6 for their M3 in 2028. This is the chip that makes local agents on battery-powered devices actually viable.

<https://www.eetimes.com/deepx-tapes-out-second-gen-chip-projects-6-7x-performance-gain-with-lpddr5x-pim/>

**[Breakout] Opus 5.5 reasoning patterns shifted November 3 — unannounced.** A user had Gemini 4 Argon analyze 1,400 Claude Code sessions and found a sharp change in Opus 5.5's reasoning behavior on November 3. Model name unchanged. No changelog. 729 points and 469 comments on HN suggests a lot of people are seeing the same thing. The model may not be "nerfed" in the traditional sense, but something changed — and the silence is what's making people nervous. When your daily driver starts behaving differently without notice, trust erodes fast.

## The Noise

**Gemini routes personal Drive/email to human annotators.** A GitHub investigation documents that Google quietly reversed its 2023 promise that Workspace-connected Gemini data "is not seen by human reviewers." The updated policy now warns users not to connect apps containing confidential information. The technical detail is interesting — Gemini's tool-calling is probabilistic, so some sessions ingest full unredacted files while others get nothing — but this is fundamentally a privacy/policy story, not an AI advancement. 341 points because people are (rightly) angry, not because it's technically novel.
